    Cross-checking

    Verify the rule used for your institution

    Every entry in this registry carries one of two confidence tiers. Understand what each means before you rely on a result.

    Official

    The institution has issued a written circular, transcript remark or conversion certificate stating this exact formula. Highest confidence, still worth a personal check if the result is going somewhere high-stakes.

    Widely reported

    Consistently used by the institution's own students, alumni and affiliated colleges but not sourced here from a single public circular. Treat as a strong estimate and confirm locally.

    Three ways to confirm the formula that applies to you

    1. Read your grade card footer. Many institutes print the conversion formula or a percentage equivalent, directly on the semester grade card or final transcript.
    2. Check the examination regulations. Your institute's academic ordinance or examination handbook — usually on the registrar's or COE's page, is the authoritative source.
    3. Ask the examination section directly. A short email to the controller of examinations asking for a "CGPA to percentage equivalence certificate" is standard practice and usually answered within a few working days.

    Questions

    Frequently asked

    Why do institutions use different formulas at all?

    There's no single UGC-mandated CGPA-to-percentage formula for higher education in India. Each university's academic council sets its own conversion when it adopts a 10-point scale, usually to keep results roughly consistent with the percentage-based system it replaced. The result is a patchwork: some use a flat ×10, others subtract an offset first, and a few use a different multiplier entirely.

    Which formula should I use on a job or higher-education application?

    Use the one your institution has actually issued to you — check your grade card or transcript first. If the form asks for a self-reported percentage and your transcript already states one, use that figure rather than recalculating it, since it's the number your institution will confirm if asked.

    My institution isn't on this list — what should I do?

    Use the report link in the verification section to send us the name and, if you have it, the formula. In the meantime, most institutions on a 10-point absolute scale that haven't published their own rule default to a straightforward CGPA × 10, but confirm with your examination section before relying on it.

    Why does the same CGPA give a different percentage in the comparison table?

    Because the multiplier and whether an offset is subtracted first, genuinely differs by institution. An 8.0 CGPA is 80% under a flat ×10 rule but only 76% under IIT Madras's ×9.5 rule and 75% under a (CGPA − 0.5) × 10 rule. None of these is "wrong" — they're just different conventions.

    Can I trust the "widely reported" entries?

    They're a reliable starting estimate built from consistent patterns across an institution's own students and affiliated colleges, but they haven't been matched against a single official circular in this registry. For anything high-stakes — an admission or a job offer — verify against your own transcript first.
